A live model of a Universal Robots UR5e that applies the kinematics, dynamics and control from RBE 501 (Robot Dynamics) and RBE 502 (Robot Control) at WPI. It uses the masses, inertias and joint limits that Universal Robots publishes for the arm.

The arm is built from the official UR5e description (Universal Robots, BSD-3-Clause): its joint origins, link masses, centres of mass, inertia tensors, joint torque limits and visual meshes. The code computes everything from those numbers while the page is open. Nothing is prerecorded.

From RBE 501

  • Forward kinematics by the product of exponentials, checked in a test against Universal Robots' published DH table.
  • The space Jacobian built from the screw axes, and the manipulability ellipsoid and measures from its eigenvalues.
  • Inverse kinematics by damped least squares, the method from Lecture 8.
  • Inverse dynamics by the recursive Newton-Euler algorithm (Lecture 18), giving the joint torques.
  • Forward dynamics, solving for joint accelerations with the inverse of the mass matrix (Lecture 20). External forces enter through \(\tau = J^{T} F\) (Lecture 12).
  • Quintic time scaling for trajectories (Lecture 19).

From RBE 502

  • The manipulator equation \(M(q)\ddot q + C(q,\dot q)\dot q + g(q) = \tau\).
  • PD control, and PD control with gravity compensation. The lectures show that PD alone settles away from the target under gravity and that adding \(g(q)\) removes that offset. The demo shows both.
  • Feedback linearization (computed torque), which cancels the nonlinear terms so that the error follows \(\ddot e + K_d \dot e + K_p e = 0\).

Planning

The planning part is not from either course. It uses RRT-Connect (Kuffner and LaValle, 2000) with shortcut smoothing. Each link is a capsule and each obstacle is a sphere. The smoothed path is time-scaled until the joint speeds and the Newton-Euler torques stay inside the UR5e limits. When a sphere is dragged across the rest of the path, the arm plans again from where it is.

How it works

Forward kinematics

$$T(\theta) = e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]\theta_1} e^{[\mathcal{S}_2]\theta_2} \cdots e^{[\mathcal{S}_6]\theta_6} M$$

The six screw axes and the home pose \(M\) come from the UR5e joint origins. The teal arrows S1 to S6 on the arm are these axes, and the meshes move by this product every frame.

Space Jacobian

$$J_{si}(\theta) = \mathrm{Ad}_{e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]\theta_1}\cdots e^{[\mathcal{S}_{i-1}]\theta_{i-1}}}\,\mathcal{S}_i$$

Column \(i\) is the screw axis of joint \(i\) in the current pose.

Manipulability

$$A = J_v J_v^{T}, \qquad \mu_3 = \sqrt{\det A}$$

The grey ellipsoid at the tool has axes along the eigenvectors of \(A\) and half-lengths \(\sqrt{\lambda_i}\). It flattens as the arm nears a singularity.

Inverse kinematics

$$\Delta\theta = J^{T}\left(JJ^{T} + \lambda^{2} I\right)^{-1} \mathcal{V}$$

Damped least squares moves the tool toward the ring you drag. The damping keeps each step finite near a singularity.

Recursive Newton-Euler

$$\mathcal{V}_i = \mathrm{Ad}_{T_{i,i-1}}\mathcal{V}_{i-1} + \mathcal{A}_i\dot\theta_i$$
$$\dot{\mathcal{V}}_i = \mathrm{Ad}_{T_{i,i-1}}\dot{\mathcal{V}}_{i-1} + \mathrm{ad}_{\mathcal{V}_i}\mathcal{A}_i\dot\theta_i + \mathcal{A}_i\ddot\theta_i$$
$$\mathcal{F}_i = \mathrm{Ad}^{T}_{T_{i+1,i}}\mathcal{F}_{i+1} + \mathcal{G}_i\dot{\mathcal{V}}_i - \mathrm{ad}^{T}_{\mathcal{V}_i}(\mathcal{G}_i\mathcal{V}_i), \qquad \tau_i = \mathcal{F}_i^{T}\mathcal{A}_i$$

The forward pass carries velocities and accelerations out to the tool, with gravity entering as \(\dot{\mathcal{V}}_0 = (0, -\mathfrak{g})\). The backward pass carries wrenches back to the base. \(\mathcal{G}_i\) holds the published mass and inertia of each link.

Equations of motion

$$M(q)\ddot q + C(q,\dot q)\dot q + g(q) = \tau$$

The simulator solves this for \(\ddot q\) with \(M(q)\) assembled from Newton-Euler and integrates it at 1 kHz. Push the arm adds a 60 N force at the end of the forearm through \(J_s^{T}\mathcal{F}\).

Controllers

$$\tau = K_p e + K_d \dot e$$
$$\tau = K_p e + K_d \dot e + g(q)$$
$$\tau = M(q)(\ddot q_d + K_d\dot e + K_p e) + C(q,\dot q)\dot q + g(q)$$

PD, PD with gravity compensation, and computed torque, with \(e = q_d - q\). PD alone sags under gravity, and the error plot shows the offset that \(g(q)\) removes.

Path planning

RRT-Connect grows one tree from the start and one from the goal in joint space. Each tree extends toward random samples and tries to connect to the other. A pose is rejected if any link capsule touches a sphere. The path is shortcut, then each segment gets the quintic time scaling \(s = 10\tau^3 - 15\tau^4 + 6\tau^5\) and is lengthened until speeds and torques stay inside the UR5e limits.

Checks

These run as tests with Node:

  • Product-of-exponentials FK matches the DH FK to 1e-9 m over 500 random poses.
  • The space Jacobian matches finite differences.
  • Newton-Euler torques match \(M(q)\ddot q + c + g\). The power \(\dot q^{T}\tau\) matches the rate of change of kinetic plus potential energy.
  • Damped least squares converges in 99 of 100 trials.
  • Every planned path is collision free when checked every 2 ms.
